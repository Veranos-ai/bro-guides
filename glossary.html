Glossary

The technical terms used on this site, in plain words.

Aliquot
A smaller portion taken from a larger amount of liquid, usually kept in its own container.
Bacteriostatic water
Sterile water with a small amount of benzyl alcohol (0.9%), which slows bacterial growth. Commonly used for vials that are opened more than once.
Cartridge
A sealed tube of liquid that fits inside a pen.
Certificate of analysis (CoA)
A laboratory document that reports test results for one batch, such as purity.
Concentration (strength)
How much peptide is in each millilitre of liquid. It is the mass divided by the volume, written as mg/mL.
Data sheet
The maker's or supplier's document giving storage and handling details for one specific peptide.
Diluent
The liquid added to a powder to dissolve it. For peptides this is usually bacteriostatic water.
Draw volume
The volume of liquid pulled into the syringe. It is the target amount divided by the strength.
Lyophilised
Freeze-dried. The water is removed under vacuum, leaving a dry powder.
mcg (microgram)
One thousandth of a milligram. 1,000 mcg equal 1 mg.
mg (milligram)
One thousandth of a gram.
Peptide
A short chain of amino acids.
Reconstitution
Adding liquid to a dry powder so it dissolves. This site calls it mixing.
Sharps container
A rigid container for used needles.
Stopper
The rubber seal on the top of a vial.
Target amount
The amount of peptide a calculation aims for. The calculator turns it into a draw volume.
U-100
A syringe standard where 100 units equal 1 mL, so 1 unit is 0.01 mL.
Unit
On a U-100 syringe, 0.01 mL of liquid. A unit measures volume, not peptide.
Vial
The small glass bottle the peptide comes in.
