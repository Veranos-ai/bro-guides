How to Store Peptides

Heat, light and repeated freezing are what spoil a peptide. This page shows what most researchers do, and the typical lifespans they report.

2 min read
Quick Answer

Most researchers move vials to the fridge as soon as they arrive. Once mixed, a vial stays in the fridge and comes out only for use. About 30 days is typical, and up to 8 weeks for robust sequences, before potency starts to decline slowly.

What Most Researchers Do

Fridge on Arrival

Unmixed vials go straight into the fridge (2–8 °C), in the box and away from light.

Mix When Needed

Once a vial is mixed, it stays in the fridge. It comes out only for use and goes straight back.

Use Inside the Window

About 30 days is typical, and up to 8 weeks for robust sequences. After that, potency declines slowly, and some researchers still use a vial beyond the window.

This is what most researchers report doing. For a specific peptide, the supplier's data sheet comes first. Common practice

After mixing (reconstituted)Fridge (2–8 °C)14 to 30 days (up to 8 weeks at most for robust sequences)
Room temperature (about 20 °C and above)24 hours to 5 days (rapid potency loss)
Frozen (aliquoted)Several months, but repeated freeze–thaw cycles are avoided

These ranges are the ones researchers commonly report. They come from experience, not from a single study. Common practice

Freezing Most researchers do not freeze-store peptides unless it is for the very long term. Freezing is not typically recommended because of the thaw cycles. Researchers who do freeze a mixed vial commonly split it into aliquots first, so each portion is thawed once.
Potency is a slope, not a cliff Potency declines gradually. The windows above mark where researchers report it starting to slip, not a date when a vial stops working.

When to Throw It Away

The liquid looks cloudy, or has bits floating in it
The colour has changed
It has been open longer than the data sheet allows
It has been frozen and thawed many times
