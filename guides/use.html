How Are Peptides Administered

This page covers how researchers commonly use and administer peptides during studies.

3 min read
Quick Answer

A fine insulin syringe or a pen holds and doses the solution, based on the concentration and required dosing.

Two Devices, One Job: Measuring Volume

Both an insulin syringe and a pen measure volume, not substance. Neither tells you how much peptide is present without the strength of the solution.

DeviceTypical specSets the volume
Insulin syringe 29–31 G, 4–8 mm, 0.5–1 mL Plunger against the scale. See reading a syringe.
Peptide pen 29–32 G needle Dial position. See how a pen works.

Commonly Used Equipment

Common practice Items researchers report keeping together:

  • Insulin syringe: 29–31 G, 4–8 mm needle, 0.5–1 mL barrel.
  • Pen: refillable or pre-filled, 29–32 G needle.
  • Alcohol pads: for the vial seal, pen seal and container lid.
  • Labelled peptide: powder reconstituted with water, or a pre-filled cartridge. The printed strength drives every calculation.
  • Sharps container: rigid and sealable, for used needles.

Administration Route: Subcutaneous Injection

The route most often described in the literature is subcutaneous: a short, fine needle into the fat layer under the skin. These third-party sources are unverified and are not instructions.

Understanding Volume vs Dose

A device only reads volume, never mass or strength. To know the concentration in mg/mL of a volume, use the reconstitution calculator.

Points of Note

  • Used needles go straight into a sharps container, sealed and taken to a collection point, never household waste.
  • If the label and the calculation disagree, stop and re-check the strength before doing anything else.
